Buying a home in Spain as a foreigner: checks before signing

Buying begins before the notary appointment: establish who is selling, what is being sold and the obligations you will accept. This is an informational sequence of questions, not an opinion on a particular apartment.

MedRealty editorial · Sources checked 2026-10-03

What to know

  1. 1. Separate identification from residence
  2. 2. Match the seller and property
  3. 3. Title checks do not replace inspection
  4. 4. Understand the agreement before payment
  5. 5. Complete notarial and registration steps

1. Separate identification from residence

The NIE identifies a foreign person dealing with Spain. The consular explanation states that a NIE obtained through a consulate does not confer residence rights or prove Spanish residence. Do not treat an identification number, a purchase contract and permission to reside as interchangeable documents.

Ask the chosen notary office for participant-document requirements in advance, including representation, translation and powers of attorney. For our organisational checklist, track document preparation, payment checks and contract review as separate deadlines. Do not rely on an intermediary's universal promise of a NIE processing time. If your aim is relocation, assess the immigration route separately with a competent professional.

  • Check names and identifiers consistently across all documents.
  • Confirm the competent consulate's or authority's requirements for your situation.

Sources: Spanish Foreign Ministry, London consulate: purpose of the NIE

2. Match the seller and property

A Property Registry nota simple reports registered rights, their holders and limitations. It is informational, not a guarantee against every possible risk. Obtain current information for the correct finca and ask your lawyer to explain unfamiliar entries.

Our reconciliation checklist compares the advertisement, address, apartment, parking, storage and seller documents. Flag discrepancies rather than resolving them through assumptions. If a representative or company sells, establish who checks their authority. Where an encumbrance appears, obtain a written plan for handling it by completion. A promise to remove it later should not replace an agreed process with clear responsibilities and evidence.

  • Who is entitled to sign the agreement?
  • Which components are included and what restrictions are recorded?

Sources: Registradores: nota simple and registered rights

3. Title checks do not replace inspection

The Registradores buyer guide recommends checking physical condition, occupants, community payments and IBI. It also highlights possible special restrictions on non-EU buyers in certain defence zones. This calls for checking the address, not assuming every foreign buyer faces a nationwide prohibition.

Prepare separate questions for a building professional about alterations, damp, equipment and visible defects. Ask your lawyer which property permissions and documents apply locally, and ask the seller for evidence supporting their answers. If letting is planned, investigate that use separately. Keep a dated record naming the person who performed each check and listing the questions still unresolved. Treat unresolved matters as questions, not automatic approval.

  • Check the property's documented use, not just photographs.
  • Do not treat a promise of tourist letting as confirmation of an acquired right.

Sources: Registradores: buyer guide, basic checks; 2020 edition

4. Understand the agreement before payment

The Consejo General del Notariado warns that a private agreement can be binding. Arras terms matter; withdrawal consequences cannot be inferred merely from an English translation calling a payment a deposit.

Send the draft to an independent lawyer before paying. In our checklist, it must clearly answer which property, price and dates are involved, who receives money, and what happens if financing fails, a defect emerges or documents are delayed. Request an explanation in a language you understand. Clarify refund and retention arrangements; do not assume any payment can be cancelled without consequences. Compare verbal promises with the actual wording.

  • Confirm the recipient's bank details through an independent channel.
  • Do not accept a provision whose consequences have not been explained.

Sources: Consejo General del Notariado: buying a home

5. Complete notarial and registration steps

The notary checks participants, documents and payment methods and explains transaction obligations. Registradores describes the separate submission of the notarial escritura and tax documentation for registration. Signing and subsequent registry work are distinct stages.

Appoint someone responsible for submission, taxes, tracking any objections and obtaining final documents. Our completion checklist also records keys, handover, payments and evidence that assigned tasks have finished. Keep the documents themselves, not only messages from an intermediary. This guide is not individual legal or tax advice: obtain independent Spanish legal advice and relevant professional checks before buying.

Sources: Consejo General del Notariado: buying a home · Registradores: registering a home purchase
